Date: 2026-08-10, AFTER the corrected `restoring` probe, the corrected K3
end-mirror, and the existence-search rewrite.
SUPERSEDES Archive/coverage-no-oar-2026-08-10.txt (39/42).

    node harness/coverage-no-oar.js --wide-search

Grid: TWA 50-180 step 10 x TWS 4/6/10 (42 points). TWA < 50 is OUT OF SCOPE
by owner decision -- deferred, not solved; see docs/README.md.

COVERAGE: 42/42 (100%) of the in-scope grid.

Single pass, 20 minutes, no stitching of partial runs and no --dense-sheet.
The same grid cost ~9 hours before the search was rewritten (see COST below).

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHAT DID NOT. No physics changed -- out/*.csv are
byte-identical and run_tests.js's byte gate is clean. Two measurement fixes
and one search rewrite:

  * K1's `restoring` term perturbed `heading` while holding BOAT-frame u/v
    fixed, which rotates the hull and the flow together, so every hull/ama
    yaw moment cancelled in the finite difference and only the rig's slope
    survived -- an order of magnitude too small and sign-flipped at TWA110
    and TWA140. Corrected in harness/asserts-helpers.js `yawMomentAtHeading`.
  * K3's end mirror flipped the heading by pi for end=-1 but not the wind.
    Not scored here (this file is end=+1 only -- see LIMITS).
  * The search now stops at the first confirmed holder (see COST).

THE THREE GAPS WERE THE PREDICATE, NOT THE BOAT AND NOT THE SEARCH. The
superseded snapshot left TWA50/60/70 at TWS6 as NONE, and N1 called them
"the strongest signal yet that these are a property of the boat" (M2's
heel-runaway mechanism). They are not. All three hold, and their winning
sheet angles are 8-12deg -- BELOW the 15deg floor of the dense grid N2 tried,
i.e. at the polar's own speed optimum, a trim every search including the
original narrow one has always contained. Those trims were being found and
then discarded at the last step by `restoring`.

Corroboration, from before the single pass replaced it: the 39 points the
old snapshot recorded as HOLDS were replayed at THEIR OWN recorded trims
under the corrected predicate, and 37 still held. The 2 that did not --
TWA180 at TWS6 and TWS10, both marginally destabilising (slope +0.3 and
+0.1, against -1.4 at TWA180/TWS4) -- hold at other trims. The near-zero
dead-run slope at all three winds is consistent with ADR 0027 ("the dead
run is unstable"): TWA180 is genuinely marginal and which side of zero it
lands on depends on the trim.

COST. Profiling one point (TWA50/TWS4) exposed what the old exhaustive pass
was spending to answer a question it had already answered: 750 trials -> 335
survivors -> 284 of those held, and the FIRST holder was survivor #2. 480 of
that point's 700 seconds went on re-proving it 283 more times. The metric
asks EXISTENCE, so the search now interleaves the 60s screen with the 300s
confirmation and breaks at the first holder, and the trial order is taken
from the 39 winning trims the old snapshot recorded (stays=+1 won 30/39,
tackX=+1 25/39, crewPosX=-1 20/39). The trial SET is unchanged, so nothing
can be hidden by reordering. TWA50/TWS4 went 700s -> 28s. What now dominates
the runtime is computePolar (~20s/point), which is real work, not waste.
  A point that does NOT hold still costs the full sweep -- non-existence
cannot be short-circuited. There are none today; expect them to dictate the
runtime of any grid that regresses.

READ THE TRIMS WITH CARE. Because the search stops at the first holder, the
trim printed below is the FIRST one found in search order, not the
minimum-excursion one. Several therefore sit close to the acceptance
thresholds (excursion <=15deg, speed >=50%): TWA170/TWS10 reports v=51%
where the old exhaustive pass found a 99% trim at the same point, and
several excursions read 14.x against the 15 ceiling. This is a property of
the REPORT, not of the boat -- every line below is a confirmed hold, and
better trims exist at most of these points. Do not read the margins here as
the boat's best.

LIMITS THIS FILE STILL HAS, unchanged by the above:
  * end=+1 ONLY. settledState hardcodes end:1, so nothing here says anything
    about the other end -- and ADR 0016/0023 record a defect that survived
    two rounds precisely because it was only visible on the un-exercised
    end. A third such defect (K3's wind mirror) was found the same day this
    file was written.
  * crewPos (the crew's LATERAL position) is frozen at the polar's
    speed-optimal value and is not a search axis -- even though M2 identified
    it as the mechanism of the beat failure, and adding it as an axis takes
    S1c from 4/6 to 6/6 on its own grid.
  * holdsCourse freezes the trim for 300s. The owner has since ruled that
    continuous re-trimming is acceptable and that the criterion is about the
    moment a course CANNOT be held, which makes this predicate stricter than
    the criterion requires -- so this number is a floor, not a ceiling.
    holdsCourseActiveTrim has been fixed but NOT re-measured.
